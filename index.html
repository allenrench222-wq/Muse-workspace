01 / FORMULA MAP

The ED279 is a six-stage pipeline.

Each section hands a result to the next

In one sentence: DOE builds Edgecomb-specific operating rates, applies them to resident pupils and weights, adds other subsidizable costs, then splits the result between the town and state according to valuation and the statewide mill expectation.

1
BUILD THE RATES

Per-pupil operating rates

Attending pupils determine model staff FTEs. State salary-matrix values, benefit factors, support costs, and the regional index produce elementary and secondary rates.

FY27: $8,698 elementary · $9,298 secondary
DOE line-by-line method ↓
2
APPLY THE RATES

Operating allocation

Resident pupils × Section 1 rates, plus economic-disadvantage and multilingual weights, targeted funds, and Edgecomb’s isolated-small-school adjustment.

FY27: $1,528,708.36
3
ADD OTHER COSTS

Other subsidizable costs

Gifted & talented, special education, transportation, teacher retirement, and debt/insured value factor are added separately.

FY27 total allocation: $2,363,258.84
4
SPLIT THE BILL

Required local and state shares

Required local is the lesser of the total allocation or state-certified valuation × the statewide mill expectation. State pays the remainder.

min($2,363,258.84, $331,933,333 × .005645)
5
ADJUST

Final allocation

DOE applies state-only items such as education service center funding and MaineCare Seed. Edgecomb’s FY27 adjusted state contribution is $484,672.53.

6
PAY

Monthly subsidy

The schedule shows monthly EPS checks, quarterly MaineCare Seed offsets, CTE subsidy, and construction aid. It is the SAU’s subsidy checkbook.

What specifically works against Edgecomb

01 · SMALL SCALE

108.5 attending pupils carry building-level fixed costs.

FY27 attendance averaged 108.5. Administration, facilities, and classroom staffing move in steps, so Edgecomb cannot shrink costs one student at a time. RSU 02 spreads its system over 1,432 subsidizable pupils and spends $1,294 less per pupil than Edgecomb.

$19,247 − $17,953 = $1,294 per pupil
02 · ABOVE-MODEL SPENDING

Scale explains pressure; 137% of EPS measures the remaining choice.

Edgecomb’s FY25 actual spending exceeded its modeled EPS level by $806,465. A 120%-of-EPS benchmark would be about $370,000 below Edgecomb’s reported level; that is a scenario, not a claim that every dollar is immediately removable.

($19,247 − $14,044) × 155 = $806,465
17% × $14,044 × 155 ≈ $370,000
03 · THIN TAX BASE PER PUPIL

The state formula helps, but each mill raises less.

Edgecomb’s FY27 valuation was $331.93M across 138 Section 4 pupils, or about $2.41M per pupil. The implied valuations behind its peers’ required-local figures run far larger — roughly $802M for Georgetown, $836M for Southport, and $2.66B for Boothbay–Boothbay Harbor. That is why Edgecomb can have AOS 98’s lowest local-share percentage and still its highest EPS mill.

$331,933,333 ÷ 138 = $2,405,314 per pupil
04 · FORMULA RELIEF IS PARTIAL

The isolated-school adjustment dents the gap; it does not erase it.

Edgecomb’s verified FY27 adjustment is $133,576.56. That equals 16.6% of the FY25 spending-above-EPS gap, but roughly 96% of Edgecomb’s gap to the statewide median. Put differently: it brings Edgecomb near median spending, not down to its own EPS allocation.

$133,576.56 ÷ $806,465 = 16.6%
($19,247 − $18,350) × 155 = $139,035
Two commonly blamed categories do not explain Edgecomb’s position.

Using DOE comparison files with a mixed-year pupil denominator, Edgecomb’s transportation allocation is approximately $506 per pupil—the lowest among the five included districts with data—and its special-education comparison amount is about $1,957 per pupil, lowest among the four AOS 98 districts. These are directional comparisons only: Edgecomb’s separate FY27 ED279 special-education line is $640,507.81, a different basis that the source record did not reconcile.

Structure explains the outliers

Georgetown & Southport

Micro-scale raises spending, but coastal valuation suppresses the EPS mill. High local-share percentages do not translate into high tax rates.

Boothbay–Boothbay Harbor CSD

One two-town SAU blends valuation, but its 166%-of-EPS spending shows that larger scale alone does not guarantee discipline.

RSU 02

District-wide averages spread costs across 1,432 pupils. At 124% of EPS and $17,953 per pupil, it is the clearest scale comparison in the requested peer set.

Great Salt Bay CSD

Its FY25 comparison remains valid, but LD 2050 dissolved the CSD by emergency enactment on March 3, 2026. The successor structure was not verified.

AOS 98

Four SAUs across five towns share superintendent and central-office services, not a tax base or a unified operating budget. Edgecomb still carries its building economics alone.

Edgecomb

It combines small-school fixed costs with materially less valuation per pupil than its coastal AOS peers. That combination—not transportation or special education—is the central disadvantage.

Three transferable lessons

  1. Track structure, not just line items. RSU 02’s cost advantage comes from multi-town scale. AOS 98 already shares administration, but deeper service sharing or reorganization is the lever that changes fixed-cost economics.
  2. Use EPS percentage as a discipline measure, not a verdict. RSU 02 operates at 124% of EPS and Great Salt Bay reported 134%; Edgecomb at 137% should separate unavoidable small-school costs from locally chosen additions before debating arbitrary cuts.
  3. Do not mistake formula aid for tax-base relief. Edgecomb already receives a larger state share than its AOS peers. The remaining pressure comes from how little valuation sits behind each pupil and from costs above the modeled EPS level.

05 / LINE BY LINE

What enters each section—and from where.

Open a section to audit its inputs
§1Computation of EPS per-pupil rates＋

Purpose: build Edgecomb-specific elementary and secondary operating rates. The result funds regular education operations only.

  • Attending pupils: two-year average of October 1 enrollment from Synergy, by grade band.
  • Model staff FTE: attending pupils ÷ EPS staffing ratio for eight staff groups. For SAUs under 1,200, example teacher ratios are 15:1 in PreK–K, 17:1 in grades 1–8, and 16:1 in grades 9–12.
  • Actual FTE: NEO Staff module, October 30 snapshot. The ratio EPS FTE ÷ actual FTE scales matrix salary.
  • Salary: each actual employee is placed on the state matrix by experience and education. Matrix salaries are summed, then scaled by percent of EPS.
  • Benefits: 26% for teachers/guidance/librarians/health; 40% for ed tech/library tech/clerical; 21% for administrators.
  • Other support: statewide per-pupil amounts for substitutes, supplies, professional development, instructional leadership, activities, system administration, and operations/maintenance.
  • Regional index: salaries + substitutes are adjusted by the labor-market index. Edgecomb’s FY27 index is 1.02.

§2Operating cost allocations＋

Purpose: apply Section 1 rates to resident pupils, then add demographic weights and targeted funds.

  • Resident pupils: Synergy October 1 records; PreK uses the most recent October, while K–8 and 9–12 use two-year averages. Residents tuitioned to another school remain included.
  • Basic FY27: 7.0 PreK × $8,698 + 89.0 K–8 × $8,698 + 42.0 grades 9–12 × $9,298 = $1,225,524.
  • Economic disadvantage: the highest reported percentage across K–8, 9–12, or total is applied with a 0.15 weight. FY27 ED279 uses 40.21%.
  • Multilingual learners: tiered weights; Edgecomb’s one K–8 learner uses 0.70: 1.0 × 0.70 × $8,698 = $6,088.60.
  • Targeted FY27 rates: assessment $58/pupil; technology $127 elementary and $382 secondary; PreK and K–2 use a 0.10 weight; disadvantaged pupils receive an additional 0.05 weight.
  • Isolated small school: computed per qualifying building; Edgecomb FY27 receives $133,576.56.

Result: $1,528,708.36 operating allocation.

§3Other subsidizable costs＋
  • Gifted & talented: lesser of audited actual or budgeted spending, inflated. FY27 uses 103.50%.
  • Special education: a formula combining base, prevalence above 15%, small-size adjustment when fewer than 20 special-ed pupils, high-cost in-district, and a maintenance-of-effort hold-harmless. High-cost out-of-district is separate and based on costs above state thresholds.
  • Transportation: prior-year allocation inflated and bounded between 90% and 105% of net operating expenditures, using the greater of pupil-density or miles-driven models.
  • Teacher retirement: a current-year normalized employer cost estimate from MainePERS.
  • Debt / IVF: state-approved principal and interest, approved leases, and private-school insured-value-factor tuition amounts, capped as described by DOE.

FY27 result: $766,475.60 other costs + $54,838.51 retirement + $13,236.37 debt service, added to Section 2.

§4Required local contribution and state share＋
  • Valuation: lesser of the most recent Maine Revenue Services certified valuation or the three-year average.
  • Mill expectation: set annually through the state budget from statewide education cost, available state resources, and statewide valuation. It is not Edgecomb’s actual property-tax rate.
  • Rule: required local contribution = lesser of total EPS allocation or valuation × mill expectation. State contribution = total allocation − required local.
min($2,363,258.84, $331,933,333 × .005645) = $1,873,763.66 local
§5Totals and adjustments＋

Section 5 starts with local and state totals, applies minimum-contributor rules where relevant, then adds state-only items. Edgecomb’s FY27 B-adjustments are +$3,919.80 for education service center membership and −$8,742.45 MaineCare Seed. The warrant-article local contribution remains $1,873,763.66.

§6Scheduled and year-to-date payments＋

The payment schedule turns the subsidy into monthly checks. Edgecomb’s FY27 monthly EPS checks are $40,768.50 in July–September and $40,263.00 in October–May ($40,263.03 in June), with MaineCare Seed offsets of $4,192.98 in July and $4,549.47 in October. The twelve checks total the $484,672.53 adjusted state contribution.

06 / BOARD-READY

Answers for the questions that recur.

Plain language, with the denominator intact
Does “$14,044 EPS per pupil” mean that is what Edgecomb spends?
No. It is FY25’s modeled EPS allocation divided by the 155 resident-pupil average: $2,176,821.11 ÷ 155. Actual FY25 spending was reported as $19,247 per pupil, or 137% of EPS. EPS is a modeled minimum-sufficient level, not a spending cap.
Does one more student cost the average per-pupil amount?
No. A per-pupil figure is an average, not marginal cost. Most staffing, building, and transportation costs move in steps. One added pupil may change the denominator and some formula weights without requiring another classroom or route.
Why is “school enrollment” lower than resident pupils?
Eddy School serves PreK–8. Edgecomb’s resident-pupil count also includes publicly funded resident high-school students tuitioned to secondary schools. Section 1 uses attending pupils; Section 2 generally uses resident pupils.
Who chose 5.645 mills?
The statewide mill expectation comes through the state budget process, based on statewide total education cost, available state resources, and statewide valuation. It caps required local contribution; it is not Edgecomb’s actual tax rate.
Is teacher retirement local salary money?
No. DOE adds MainePERS’s normalized employer-cost estimate to EPS, and the SAU remits that amount to the retirement system. It is shown in the allocation but is not ordinary local salary spending.
Why can actual spending exceed EPS?
Actual salaries and contracts can differ from the state matrix; small schools cannot achieve every modeled scale efficiency; high-school tuition is substantial; and towns may approve additional local spending. LD 318 reports a statewide median of 134% of EPS.
07 / WHAT CHANGES

The 2026 overhaul starts in FY28.

LD 2226 · phased protection through FY2030

The enacted changes update three parts of the model beginning in 2027–28. Exact Edgecomb values cannot be known until DOE publishes the FY28 calculation.

STEP 1

County cost-of-living index

The 35 labor-market areas frozen since FY2005 are replaced by county-based indices, with a 1.00 floor. Edgecomb’s FY27 index is 1.02; its future Lincoln County value is still to be determined.

STEP 2

Poverty-weighted local mill

The local mill will reflect 90% property valuation and 10% student-poverty measure. It redistributes local and state shares without changing the statewide total. The research indicates Edgecomb would likely move downward, but the exact result is not yet published.

STEP 3

Three-year hold-harmless

Through FY2030, an SAU’s state subsidy may not fall below the prior year because of Steps 1–2. Edgecomb is protected from a formula-driven subsidy loss during the phase-in.

08 / AUDIT NOTES

What remains open or ambiguous.

Do not overstate these points
01

FY25 ED279 is preliminary. The DOE portal report located for FY25 is dated April 23, 2025 and labeled “Governor’s Supplemental Budget.” A final certified FY25 portal report was not located.

02

Part of FY25 Section 2 was truncated in the live read. The operating total is implied as $1,591,328.20 by Section 3 arithmetic, but the disadvantaged-targeted lines and isolated-small-school line were not directly captured.

03

Do not mix demographic columns or years. Edgecomb’s FY25 ED279 uses 45.10% economic disadvantage and LD 318 rounds that to 45%; FY26 uses 52.53% and FY27 uses 40.21%. The 21% shown in LD 318 is the special-education rate, not economic disadvantage.

04

Librarian lines remain unusual. FY26 reported 0.0 actual librarian and library-tech FTE while showing $6,215 and $5,503 adjusted salaries; FY27 again reports 0.0 actual FTE, this time with $0 matrix salary. Treat those figures as reported; the underlying display logic is not fully explained.

05

FY28 Edgecomb effects are not yet final. The new Lincoln County index and poverty-weighted mill will be set in future DOE calculations. Directional language is not a substitute for the published FY28 ED279.

06

FY27 figures are actuals, but preliminary. The peer table now uses ED279 Section 5A amounts from DOE’s FY27 GPA comparison (March 3, 2026), replacing the earlier LD 2226 baseline estimates. The FY27 printouts are labeled “Governor’s Recommended Funding Level,” and DOE may adjust them during FY27. Great Salt Bay has no row because the CSD was dissolved; its successor structure was not verified.

07

Transportation and special-education peer rates are directional. They divide FY26 or January 2025 allocations by FY25 pupil counts. Edgecomb’s special-education summary-file amount and its FY27 ED279 amount use different bases that were not reconciled.

08

Some FY27 lines are calculated, not printed. The DOE portal capture cut off the ED279’s right-hand product column, so the Section 1 teacher line, the Section 2 weighted/targeted split, and the transportation line are calculated here from the report’s own printed inputs — the weighted and targeted lines are combined for that reason, and transportation is the reconciling remainder after gifted & talented and special education. Every section total, and every other figure in the worked example, is the report’s printed value, and all identities reconcile exactly.
